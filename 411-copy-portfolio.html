"Why I got rid of my fabric stash"

Role: Concept, hooks, script & captions · Angle: problem/upgrade · Built for a UGC creator

Hook variations (built for testing)

  • Why I got rid of my fabric stash… (winning hook)
  • Say goodbye to this old stash!
  • Don't you HATE when this happens?
Script
Why I got rid of my fabric stash… I finally found a supplier for high quality fabrics! It's the Mrs. Quilty Monthly Kit. Now I get 16 pieces of premium cotton fabrics with vibrant, perfectly matched patterns every month. I never worry about faded colors or mismatched patterns. The kit comes with easy instructions, so everything's curated for me — quilting is easier and more fun. Join thousands of happy quilters. Subscribe now and get two FREE gifts!
Why it works: opens on a specific, felt frustration — not the product. The viewer who owns a "fabric stash" is instantly qualified, and the box is framed as the upgrade to a problem they already have, so it can appear early without feeling like an ad. Four hooks were written from day one so the same script could be tested against different pattern-interrupts.

How I think about testing & measurement

  • Hooks first. The hook is ~80% of a video ad's performance, so it's variable #1 — 4–5 hooks against one body, isolated so I know what actually moved the number.
  • One angle ≠ one audience. The same product gets multiple emotional angles (upgrade, calm, belonging) run in parallel — the algorithm tells me which one it can scale.
  • Read the funnel, not the vanity. Hook rate judges the hook, hold rate judges the body, and CPL / cost-per-acquisition is the only number that decides what lives, scales, or dies.
  • Diagnose, then reproduce. When one ad wins, the job isn't to celebrate — it's to name why (the mechanic, not the luck) and rebuild the next batch off it. That's how a winner becomes a system.
